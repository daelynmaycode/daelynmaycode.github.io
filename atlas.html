03 / 07 World Atlas

Sixteen square kilometres of the same geography.

The original 256 × 256 overworld is decoded straight from the ROM and mapped at 15.625 metres per reference tile. Coastlines, forests and mountain ranges keep their exact relative positions; only the distance between them changes.

Footprint 4,000 × 4,000 m 8 settlements · 6 regions · 16 landmarks Layout approved 12 Nov 2026

The map is not an imitation

Before any 3D geometry existed, the project decoded the overworld's run-length encoded rows out of PRG bank 1 of the original cartridge. Tile types, forest flags, encounter flags, teleport flags and walkability were read from PRG bank 0.

That decoded grid is the source of the landscape you can walk across. The 3D terrain is not modelled by hand, and it is not an artist's impression of the map — it is a height field generated from the same bytes that produced the 1987 coastline.

The image to the right is the independently decoded overworld, rendered from the ROM data alone. It predates the 3D build and remains its geometry reference.

03.1 Regions

Six overlays, not loading zones.

Regions are organisational. They overlap by design, because the original continent does not divide into neat boxes.

Region D is a constraint, not a label. The central kingdoms must contain forests, rivers, valleys, farmland, small villages, ruins, roads, trails and mountain passes. It is explicitly forbidden from becoming uninterrupted grassland — the 4 km scale is only defensible if the space between canonical places is authored.

03.3 Coneria

The one town built from its own plan.

Every wall run, gate, street, canal and plot in the capital is placed from coordinates read off the original town map — 0.21 metres per plan pixel.

Built from the map, not around it

The earlier version of Coneria was a 500 × 400 m rectangle with four gatehouses and a circular fountain. It was replaced: the wall is now an irregular stepped perimeter traced from the reference, split by a north gate and a south gate that both sit on the same avenue.

Castle on its own ground

Castle Coneria keeps its approved 180 × 160 m curtain north of the town, separated by roughly 104 metres of open grass and a paved approach. The town itself moved 60 m south so the castle could keep its position.

Seven plots, one landmark

A north-west residential pair, a north-east house, a weapons and armour pair, a mid-right house, and the large south-western inn with its INN board — each on its own grass plot, with conifer rows inside the north wall.

Solid from the first frame

A 216 × 166 m collision slab sits under the town and another under the castle court, so the ground is walkable before terrain streaming completes. The town slab sits below the canal beds, which keeps the channels at their intended depth.

03.5 Field craft

The space between the landmarks.

Distances are specified so that a 4 km world reads as landscape rather than a stretched tile map.

Scale targets

  • Main roads 6–10 m
  • Secondary roads 3–6 m
  • Dirt paths 1.5–3 m
  • Small villages 100–180 m
  • Large forests 300–700 m
  • Major rivers 15–40 m
  • Large lakes 200–500 m
  • Mountain ranges 300–900 m

Roads near Coneria are maintained. Near Melmond they deteriorate. Near Elfheim they dissolve into the forest.

Permitted narration

Additive content

Farming settlements, abandoned villages, watchtowers, ruined castles, roadside inns, forest shrines, graveyards, mines and hidden waterfalls may fill the gaps between canonical places.

Hard rule

Nothing displaces a landmark

Additive content must respect the original geography and timeline. Moving or resizing a town requires re-running the layout validator and re-checking every original landmark distance.

Single source

Data is canonical

world_layout.json is the authority for every coordinate and footprint. The written specification explains it; the JSON decides it. The validator reports zero errors.
