GTA VI Should Not Try to Be GTA V Again

GTA V's achievement — three protagonists, a satire of the American dream, a decade-long online afterlife — is complete and unrepeatable. GTA VI's opportunity is not a bigger GTA V but a different one: a systemic Leonida where the world, not the script, is the star.

Everything Rockstar has actually shown points that way: individually art-directed NPCs, an information-driven wanted system, wildlife and weather as systems rather than set dressing. The studio is building a place that reacts. Chasing GTA V's tone beat-for-beat would waste that machinery on nostalgia.

The risk is real — audiences arrive expecting Los Santos with palm trees. But the boldest thing Rockstar can do with the most expensive game ever made is trust its simulation and let Leonida surprise people, the way San Andreas and Liberty City once did.

OPN-002 SEP 2026 EDITORIALOPINION

Why the 30 FPS Lock Is a Defensible Trade

Preview coverage confirms GTA VI targets 30 FPS on consoles at launch — and the reaction was predictable. But frame rate is a budget, and Rockstar has spent it where it compounds: simulation density, NPC variety, draw distance across a 2×-GTA-V map, and systemic interactions that all run on the CPU.

An open-world game's costliest work is not pixels but decisions per second — every pedestrian, driver, cop, and animal thinking at once. Doubling the frame budget would mean halving the world budget. For a game whose entire pitch is a living Leonida, that trade runs the wrong direction.

None of this excuses a sloppy implementation: frame pacing must be rock-solid, and a 60 FPS performance mode may well arrive later, as Rockstar's history suggests. But at launch, 30 dense frames beat 60 empty ones. Judge the world first, the counter second.
